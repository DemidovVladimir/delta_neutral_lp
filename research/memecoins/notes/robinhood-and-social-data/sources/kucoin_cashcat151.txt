CASHCAT Listed on Robinhood, Surges 151% in 15 Hours
Author: Claude, Shenchao TechFlow
DeepTide Summary: Robinhood officially launched trading for the meme coin CASHCAT on August 6. Named after Robinhood’s former name “Cash Cat,” it is the highest-market-cap token on its proprietary L2 chain. Within 15 hours of launch, the price surged 151% to $0.214 before giving back most of those gains. On-chain analytics firm Bubblemaps detected that two days before the announcement, a group of newly created wallets collectively purchased millions of dollars worth of CASHCAT, now holding unrealized profits exceeding $3 million—suggesting possible advance knowledge of the listing.
Robinhood has opened the door to its largest on-chain meme coin.
On August 6, Robinhood launched trading for CASHCAT simultaneously on its mobile app and desktop Legend platform. The token’s name originates from a piece of Robinhood’s history: founders Vlad Tenev and Baiju Bhatt initially named their 2010 startup “Cash Cat” before rebranding to Robinhood. Someone turned this abandoned name into a meme coin and deployed it on Robinhood’s proprietary L2 chain, which went live on July 1 of this year.
As soon as the listing announcement was made, CASHCAT surged from $0.085 to $0.214, rising 151% in 15 hours. Within the most intense hour, the price jumped 66%, with a single-hour trading volume of $17.2 million. However, the gains quickly reversed, and the price has since retreated to around $0.12, with a market cap of approximately $117 million.
The issue is that CASHCAT has dropped from its high point several weeks ago.
New wallet built a large position two days early, with unrealized profits exceeding $3 million.
On August 6, on-chain analytics firm Bubblemaps posted about a suspicious pattern: a group of newly created wallets collectively purchased millions of dollars worth of CASHCAT two days before Robinhood officially announced its launch. After the announcement, these wallets had unrealized profits exceeding $3 million.
A previous post by Bubblemaps (August 4) provided more specific details: a cluster of new wallets purchased $2.7 million in CASHCAT the previous night, after which the token doubled in value. Bubblemaps directly posed the question: “Coordinated?”
Bubblemaps did not attribute ownership of the wallets or directly accuse any party. Robinhood has not commented on these addresses.
According to BeInCrypto, Bubblemaps’ on-chain bubble map reveals financial connections between these new wallets, with multiple wallets exhibiting clustered fund movement patterns. While such patterns are not uncommon in the meme coin space, the timing—coinciding precisely with a major listing announcement—makes the indication more pointed.
The company issued a token under its former name and listed it on its own exchange.
What makes CASHCAT unique is its ambiguous relationship with Robinhood.
This token has no team and no roadmap. The official website’s FAQ states plainly: “We have no affiliation with Robinhood. We simply thought ‘Cash Cat’ was a great name they shouldn’t have abandoned—it’s fan fiction with some code added.”
But Robinhood’s stance has been inconsistent. On July 2, Vlad Tenev told CNBC that “assets without utility have no lasting value,” emphasizing RWA as the way forward. Six days later, he posted on X that Robinhood Chain is “also great for memes” and followed the CASHCAT account. On July 30, during the Q2 earnings call, traders noticed that when Tenev demonstrated the mobile app, CASHCAT appeared in his recent search history—the token rose 16% that day.
From a fan-made novel to being officially listed for trading on Robinhood, CASHCAT has made the leap from a grassroots token to "quasi-official recognition." Bitstamp, Robinhood’s subsidiary exchange, also listed CASHCAT on the same day without issuing a separate listing announcement.
A meme coin moves an entire chain: $518 million in daily trading volume
On its launch day, Robinhood Chain's cross-chain DEX trading volume reached $518 million, a 60% increase from the previous day. Total on-chain deposits amounted to approximately $433 million. A single memecoin drove unprecedented trading activity across the entire chain.
When this chain launched on July 1, its positioning was as a dedicated chain for tokenized stocks and RWA. Robinhood spent over a year building it, launching 95 tokenized stocks. However, on-chain activity was dominated by meme coins from the first week. According to a Bernstein research report on July 13, the total value of tokenized RWA was only about $12.8 million, while meme coin weekly trading volume had already reached billions of dollars during the same period. By late July, RWA value had grown to approximately $70 million, but trading in meme coins and stablecoins remained overwhelmingly dominant.
Previous statistics from Bubblemaps were even more alarming: among the top 50 meme coins on Robinhood Chain, 63% of 164,000 traders were at a loss, while only 37% were profitable. In stark contrast, the earliest buyers saw astonishing gains. One wallet purchased CASHCAT within 30 minutes of its launch for $85, with its peak paper value reaching $2.3 million—a return of approximately 27,000 times. Another wallet bought at $316 and still holds a position worth over $2.17 million.
Additionally, CASHCAT has seen a 30-day price increase of approximately 1,193%, but it remains about 48% below its all-time high of $0.229 on July 11. The main trading pool has only about $5.3 million in liquidity, while trading volume on its listing day was 17 times the liquidity—indicating that large sell orders could significantly impact the price.
On-chain data shows that approximately 48,710 wallets currently hold CASHCAT. Listing on Robinhood’s main platform significantly lowers the entry barrier for retail investors; whether buying demand matches selling depth will determine if the current price level can be sustained.
Previously, Bubblemaps conducted a position distribution analysis on CASHCAT and concluded that "no major position clusters were detected; the distribution is relatively dispersed." The early concentrated position clusters have already been liquidated.
This contrasts with tokens like CASHDOG, which are labeled as "highly bundled." However, before FOMO sets in, players should consider whether this new pattern of concentrated purchases in new wallets prior to listing constitutes a new form of concentrated holding—do not be swayed by lowered entry barriers or the allure of the "favorite child" narrative and go all-in.
