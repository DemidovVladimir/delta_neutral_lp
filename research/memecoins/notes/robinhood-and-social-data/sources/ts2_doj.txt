Robinhood Engineers Charged Over Hyperliquid Trades; HOOD Faces a Listing-Control Test Skip to content Advertisements Advertisements Federal prosecutors have charged two former Robinhood engineers with commodities fraud and wire fraud, alleging they used confidential token-listing information to buy perpetual futures on Hyperliquid before Robinhood Crypto announced the listings. The alleged gains—more than $50,000 for each defendant—are small beside Robinhood’s revenue. For HOOD shareholders, the harder issue is how employees with authorized access allegedly monetized information the company treated as commercially sensitive. The Southern District of New York unsealed complaints on Sept. 15 against Hefu Chai, 36, and Huaisong “Jerry” Xiang, 30. Each faces one count under the Commodity Exchange Act and one count of wire fraud. The charges are accusations; both defendants are presumed innocent unless convicted. What the complaints allege Both engineers worked on Robinhood’s crypto-listing process. According to prosecutors, access to upcoming listings was limited to “Coin Aware Individuals” in a private Slack channel. Robinhood’s policy barred those employees from trading the relevant assets on Robinhood or any other venue before an announcement and for 24 hours afterward. Advertisements The complaint against Chai says he was a technical lead responsible for adding digital assets and traded ahead of at least 10 announcements. It describes long positions in perpetual futures tied to tokens including MEW, MOODENG, HYPE, AERO and SYRUP. Prosecutors say the trades were placed through wallets linked to an exchange account in Chai’s name. Perpetual futures track an asset’s price without an expiry date. Robinhood was not the trading venue in the alleged scheme: the positions were opened on Hyperliquid, a decentralized derivatives exchange. That difference is central to the case. Prosecutors are arguing that taking confidential information from an employer and using it in crypto derivatives can support commodities-fraud and wire-fraud charges even when the trades occur away from the employer’s platform. The complaints say a token could become tradable on Robinhood as much as an hour before the public announcement, and that availability often created upward price pressure. They also describe the listing calendar as commercially valuable because rival exchanges could add the same assets and customers could trade elsewhere if the plans leaked early. Advertisements Robinhood told The Block that it investigated the matter, reported it to law enforcement and regulators, and is cooperating. The Justice Department separately thanked Robinhood for its cooperation. That response is a material counterweight: the company is not charged, and the complaints do not allege that customer assets were stolen. The control question is larger than the alleged profit Robinhood reported $100 million of cryptocurrency transaction revenue in the second quarter, down 38% from a year earlier. Crypto supplied about 7.6% of the company’s $1.31 billion in total net revenue, based on its quarterly filing . Robinhood App and Bitstamp crypto notional volume totaled $40 billion for the period. At the stated minimum, the two alleged gains add up to just over $100,000, roughly 0.008% of one quarter’s net revenue. That comparison shows why the immediate financial cost is not the point. A listing calendar can move thinly traded tokens, and Robinhood’s expanding crypto business depends on customers and counterparties believing its employees cannot trade ahead of them. The company’s defense starts with detection and escalation. If Robinhood’s own controls identified the conduct quickly and produced the records that tied trades to the defendants, the case may demonstrate that monitoring worked. The more serious outcome would be evidence that managers ignored alerts, that access was broader than necessary, or that similar trading occurred without detection. None of those failures is alleged in the Sept. 15 release. ▶ HOOD closed at $104.42 on Sept. 16, down 5.5% in the regular session, according to Yahoo Finance market data . The charges became public the previous day, but the move cannot be assigned to one headline: U.S. shares were also digesting a Federal Reserve rate increase and a broad risk-off session. Advertisements The next useful evidence will come from process changes, not the criminal penalties. A Robinhood disclosure on tighter access logs, employee-wallet surveillance or the gap between a token going live and its public announcement would show remediation. An additional case, a civil enforcement action or evidence of ignored alerts would widen the risk beyond the two defendants.
