Haseeb Qureshi: Robinhood Chain's $18 Million Days Are a Weekend Arbitrage Machine, Not a New Onboarding Engine — BigGo Finance Haseeb Qureshi: Robinhood Chain's $18 Million Days Are a Weekend Arbitrage Machine, Not a New Onboarding Engine Add preferred source Robinhood's Ethereum Layer 2 has become the most revenue-generative chain in crypto, processing $1.4 billion in daily DEX volume and flipping Solana for the top spot by revenue — all driven by a memecoin-to-tokenized-stock trading meta that Dragonfly Capital managing partner Haseeb Qureshi calls "financial jackassery." In a wide-ranging interview on Bankless, Qureshi argued that the chain's median fees run 128x higher than Solana's and twice Ethereum L1's, yet Ethereum captures only 0.014% of the take — comparing the relationship to a "dollarized economy" rather than a tax-paying colony. He cautioned that the revenue dominance may prove temporary, noting how memecoin manias have risen and fallen on other chains like Base. The conversation also covered Solana's narrowly approved issuance cut, which Qureshi framed not as a store-of-value pivot but as evidence of the weakening digital asset treasury ecosystem. Beyond crypto, he weighed in on the widening Fed-Treasury rift over bond yields, Strategy's procyclical Bitcoin trading, a 153-million-record KYC breach, and OpenAI's new Astra model released in the shadow of what he called "the Fukushima for AI nerds." The through-line: governance and accountability at scale — whether in Layer 2 economics, monetary policy, or AI safety — is the defining test of this cycle. Key Elements Robinhood Haseeb Qureshi Solana Ethereum Strategy OpenAI Federal Reserve Kraken Artificial Inu AMC Entertainment Circle Anthropic The first week of September 2026 finds crypto in a strange kind of split-screen. On one side, a single Ethereum Layer 2 chain is processing $1.4 billion in daily DEX volume and generating $18 million in revenue on a single day — more than Ethereum itself. On the other side, the engine of that boom is a memecoin-to-tokenized-stock trading loop that Dragonfly Capital managing partner Haseeb Qureshi, speaking on Bankless, did not hesitate to label "financial jackassery." The numbers are genuinely staggering. Robinhood Chain, launched in early July, has accumulated nearly $20 billion in cumulative volume in under two months. Its median transaction fee runs at twice Ethereum L1's level and 128 times Solana's. Annualized on a 7-day basis — a method Qureshi explicitly cautioned against — the chain's revenue would surpass Robinhood's entire equity trading business for the year. The breakout example involved a memecoin called "Boner," paired against HIMS, the direct-to-consumer erectile dysfunction medication company. The pairing created a feedback loop where memecoin traders could effectively trade a leveraged, 24/7 version of the underlying stock — at least until the arbitrage window slammed shut. "The problem with a meme coin paired with a stock taking off on a weekend is that there's no arbitrage you can do on the weekend. There's no way to move the real price of the stock up... people thought they had these huge runners... And then on Monday morning once the market opens, people realize, 'Oh wait, this thing has diverged from the underlying stock by like 400%.'" The Weekend Pump, the Monday Dump The pattern is predictable and, to Qureshi, predatory. Memecoin runners spike over weekends when traditional markets are closed. When Monday arrives, hedge funds mint the underlying stock and sell into the inflated token price, crushing late entrants. The only guaranteed winners are the memecoin boosters who get paid upfront and the funds executing the arbitrage. The spillover has now reached actual micro-cap stocks. Qureshi pointed to a memecoin named "Farmy," tied to a Chinese dried-mushroom company with 15 employees and a market cap near $5–10 million that was at risk of NASDAQ delisting. When the token ran, the actual stock rose 300–350%. "We have fallen a long way that we're now like playing games with $5 million micro caps... We used to have memecoin runners that would go to like a billion and now we're like, 'Oh, wow. We moved a $5 million Chinese mushroom company that's at risk of getting delisted on the NASDAQ.' This is obviously not good for people to be doing." Reference data underscores the scale of the phenomenon. The chain's DEX volume hit a record $989 million on August 28, with total value locked climbing to roughly $708 million — nearly double the prior month. Launch platforms like PONS saw market caps rise tenfold in August, while tokens like Artificial Inu, paired against tokenized Nvidia stock, surged from $1.5 million to a $135 million peak in 30 days. A single wallet, opened just three weeks prior, converted $2,972 into over $2.1 million on a token called MEME — a 713x return driven by a dispute between AMC Entertainment CEO Adam Aron and Robinhood over tokenized stock trading. Dune dashboard data shows most Robinhood Chain activity comes from existing crypto natives migrating from other chains — FOMO and GMGN users, Qureshi noted, who are chain-agnostic. TVL remains roughly one-eighth of Base's despite generating more volume. Whether this becomes a genuine onboarding event depends on whether the mania grows by another order of magnitude. Qureshi's prediction: don't count on it. Memecoin manias have risen and fallen on other chains before. The Dollarized Economy: What Robinhood Chain Means for Ethereum The chain's success raises uncomfortable questions about Ethereum's economic model. Robinhood Chain runs on Ethereum's stack, uses ETH for fees, and is interoperable with the broader ecosystem. Yet Ethereum captures almost none of the revenue. On one representative day, Robinhood Chain generated $18 million; Ethereum's take was $155,000 — a take rate of 0.014%. Even Arbitrum, which supplies the underlying technology, receives only a 10% cut. Qureshi offered a geopolitical analogy to set expectations: "It's almost like, if we have a satellite state, the US has a satellite state that uses the dollar. It's a dollarized economy. We're not getting tax revenue from countries that dollarize, but we do increase exports of the dollar and we increase the demand for the dollar globally. I think that's the right mental model for what Robin Hood chain is to Ethereum." The framing matters. Ethereum should not expect to tax its L2s like colonies; the benefit flows through increased demand for ETH as the native asset and expanded reach of the Ethereum economic zone. Qureshi was direct: Robinhood Chain is "part of the Ethereum economic zone, but it's not directly paying taxes. It's not a colony. It's not like this isn't, you know, the 51st state of Ethereum or something like that." For Solana, the threat is more direct. Robinhood Chain has flipped it for revenue, and much of Solana's recent growth had been memecoin-driven. Solana Cuts Issuance — But Not for the Reason You Think Solana just executed its first binding on-chain governance vote to reduce issuance, accelerating the timeline to its 1.5% annual floor from 2032 to early 2029. The move saves an estimated 19 million SOL — roughly $2 billion at current prices — that would have gone to validators. The vote barely passed, requiring 67% approval and clearing the threshold by just 33 basis points. Kraken's validators initially voted no before flipping in the final hours, a move that pushed the proposal across the finish line after what one of the proposal's authors described as "500 calls in the past few hours." Qureshi argued this should not be read as Solana pivoting to a store-of-value strategy. Instead, it reflects the collapse of the Solana DAT — digital asset treasury — ecosystem that had been the primary constituency for high nominal yields. Let's just lower inflation and let's hope that that means that it's going to be better for asset prices and less tax leakage." The governance mechanism itself is a point of contrast with Ethereum. Solana's stake-weighted on-chain voting — where 67% of validators can change monetary policy directly — represents what Vitalik Buterin has historically opposed: enshrined plutocracy. Ethereum relies on rough social consensus rather than direct democracy on monetary matters. "If we voted on rates as a country, we would just vote rates down and down and down and down and down and we'd probably result in massive inflation... To save us from ourselves, we don't have that system." Qureshi's view: both approaches are valid for their respective networks. Ethereum positions itself as a store of value with deliberate conservatism; Solana aims to be the faster, more corporate "NASDAQ for blockchains." The ecosystem benefits from this diversity of governance experiments. The Fed, the Treasury, and the AI Growth Bet The macro backdrop is increasingly tense. The 30-year Treasury yield has risen back above 5.26%, approaching 20-year highs, despite Treasury Secretary Bessent's ongoing bond-buyback program. The 10-year yield sits at 20-month highs. The dynamic between Bessent and Fed Chair Waller has become openly adversarial. Waller's Jackson Hole speech was interpreted as hawkish, with markets pricing a September rate hike at 67% before pulling back. The key line: "We must be confident that underlying inflation is moving to our objective clearly and at sufficient speed. Otherwise, we have work to do." "The White House wants to do things that are good for the economy... They want to ride the edge. And at the end of the day, Waller, yeah, he was appointed by Trump, but now he's independent. And you can see that independence starting to show its colors." Bessent, speaking at the G20 summit, dismissed bond market concerns as media distraction, pointing to the "American economic boom": AI superpower status, energy superpower status, record corporate earnings, and GDP growth. He claimed the US has "the best performing bond market among major countries in the world" — a statement Qureshi characterized as "being the skinniest fat kid," since all global bonds are being punished for fiscal irresponsibility. The deeper issue is that both the administration and the Fed appear to be making a coordinated bet on AI as the solution to fiscal challenges — growing the economy fast enough to outrun debt concerns. Qureshi's counterpoint: a booming AI-driven economy is precisely why government bonds are less attractive. Investors prefer direct exposure to AI companies over lending to a government that must tax those companies to service its debt.
