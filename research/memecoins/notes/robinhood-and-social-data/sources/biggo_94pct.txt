Solana Meme Coin Carnage: 94% of Traders Lose Money Over 90 Days
The meme coin boom on Solana has turned into a brutal reality check for retail traders, with fresh on-chain data revealing that nearly 94% of active wallets lost money over the past 90 days. Of the 304,161 traders tracked on Solana decentralized exchanges, only 19,003 finished the period in profit, according to data compiled by FOMO and Dune Analytics.
The median trader posted a loss of $120, a stark illustration of how value has flowed away from the vast majority of participants even as the sector continues to attract new users. Among the fortunate few who did make money, the gains were often modest: 88% of profitable wallets earned less than $100.
The figures paint a picture of a market that has largely become a peer-to-peer extraction mechanism rather than a venue for outsized returns. On-chain research estimates that the group of Solana meme coin traders lost approximately $1.26 billion in aggregate, based on observed valuations. The profitability metric tracks realized profits and losses rather than paper valuations from holding tokens, which helps account for the discrepancy — many meme tokens see their liquidity evaporate within hours or days.
A Handful of Winners in a Sea of Losses
The distribution of profits is extraordinarily concentrated. Only 25 wallets out of more than 304,000 tracked accounts generated realized gains exceeding $10,000 over the three-month period. That is not 25% of traders — it is 25 individual wallets.
The FOMO app, which allows newcomers to copy the trades of certain influencers, provides another lens on the same phenomenon. The platform's data shows the same pattern: a tiny elite of profitable accounts amid mass losses. The copy-trading model has made it easier for inexperienced users to enter the market, but it has also led to inflated valuations for several meme tokens as followers pile in behind influencers, often too late to capture the initial surge.
The spectacular gains that circulate on social media platform X represent only a small fraction of the overall market. For the majority of participants, the experience is one of rapid losses.
Pump.fun Thrives as Traders Struggle
While individual traders have struggled, the infrastructure layer of the meme coin economy continues to generate significant revenue. Pump.fun, the Solana-based token launchpad, recorded approximately $4.4 million in daily fees on August 19, its best level since January. The platform ranked among the top three protocols by fee generation on that day.
The contrast between platform profitability and trader losses underscores the asymmetry of the current meme coin market. Pump.fun has adjusted its remuneration system multiple times, including a change in February that aimed to favor meme coin traders more directly. Yet the fundamental economics remain challenging for users.
The meme coin lifecycle on Solana has also accelerated dramatically. New tokens are launched continuously, with many making no attempt to build a community over months. Instead, they exist for a few hours or days, attract speculative interest, and then vanish. The barrier to entry for token creation has dropped so low that it can take only minutes to create and begin trading a new asset. Finding a buyer willing to pay more remains the difficult part.
The meme coin phenomenon has extended beyond Solana. Robinhood saw significant engagement from its fast meme token launch feature, with meme coins representing up to 51% of activity on new listings at certain points. However, that trend has begun to reverse. As of August 19, Robinhood-listed meme tokens erased roughly 10% of their value overnight, with losses exceeding 30% over the preceding week.
The broader meme coin sector has also experienced turbulence. In June, established tokens including Dogecoin, Shiba Inu, and Pepe were caught up in a sharp correction that rippled across the category.
New platforms continue to emerge despite the grim statistics. Crypto influencer Ansem recently launched a platform that incentivizes usage through airdrops, though the trading activity itself may produce similar loss patterns. As with other launchpads, users are effectively absorbing losses on the vast majority of meme launches while waiting for the occasional breakout token.
The data suggests that meme coins remain one of the few crypto sectors still attracting significant new user inflow, largely because launches happen entirely on-chain with no listing delays or centralized exchange gatekeeping. Newcomers can bypass Bitcoin and blue-chip tokens entirely. But the on-chain data from the past 90 days indicates that for most of them, the entry into meme trading has been an expensive education.
